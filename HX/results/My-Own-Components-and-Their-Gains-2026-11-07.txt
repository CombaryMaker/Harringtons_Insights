MY OWN COMPONENTS AND WHAT EACH ONE ADDS
Michael Hugh Harrington - 7 November 2026
Only components that are in my provisional patent (U.S. Provisional App. 64/192,843, Patent Pending) or already open
source on my GitHub are listed. Every result was decompressed and checked byte for byte. "Smaller" means the
compressed file is that much smaller than the comparison named on the same line, on the same data.

The standard tools (zstd, LZMA, Brotli, BSC, bzip3, PPMd, JPEG XL and so on) are other people's work and are credited
as such. What follows is the part that is mine: what I put around those tools, in front of them or in place of them,
and what it measurably adds.


PART A - HX (PATENT PENDING)

1. Harrington Exchange lines
   What it does: reshapes each piece of a file before coding (splitting numbers into byte lanes, predicting pixels,
   sound and table columns, renaming common values) and trials many such views quickly to keep the best.
   Gains:
   - Biased data: within 0.06-0.31% of the theoretical entropy floor, where Huffman, FSE and rANS alone are 1.7-43%
     above it.
   - NIST SP 800-90B noise: the same size as lpaq1 -9, but decodes in 0.129 s against 19.4 s (150x faster).
   - Medical DICOM: 3.15x at 0.035 s decode, against bzip3 3.10x at 0.33 s (smaller and 9x faster to open).
   - Packed 12-bit biased data: 11.8% smaller than the best other method (324,507 vs 368,027 bytes).
   - NIST biased bits: 9.4% smaller than xz (19,722 vs 21,772 bytes).

2. Class-sorted layout (Harrington's Sets inside HX)
   What it does: groups the parts of a file that look alike, so each group is coded with its own statistics, with
   a tiny map to put everything back.
   Gains:
   - Data that changes type every 1 MB: 9.0% smaller than plain blocks (measured independently by Andrew Goodwin;
     zstd --long gained only 0.5% on the same file).
   - Mixed bank backup: 1.4% smaller than plain blocks (17,247,784 vs 17,497,009 bytes).
   - Mixed 3.2 GB archive: 0.4% smaller than plain blocks, and 1.27x faster to decode than single-stream zstd.

3. Similarity ordering within a group (the H Processor)
   What it does: lines up the pieces inside each group so every piece sits next to the one most like it. It is
   kept only when the file actually gets smaller, map included.
   Gains on a 113 MB public packet capture:
   - zstd -3: 9.6% smaller.
   - Brotli 11: 9.4% smaller.
   - HCM: 8.9% smaller, and 13% faster to decode.
   - LZ4 HC: 8.1% smaller.
   - BSC: 4.5% smaller.
   - Silesia xml: 1.8% smaller with HCM.

4. Harrington Cascades
   What it does: passes leftovers down successive lines. Noise-looking pieces are pooled so copies find each other,
   and only the final remainder is stored as random.
   Gains (against my published Sets version):
   - Hidden 16-bit pattern: 39.5% smaller.
   - Repeated random chunks: 41.1% smaller with zstd, 46.0% smaller with context mixing.
   - Content-switching file: 18.0% smaller with context mixing.
   - True random data: recognised and stored at once (1 s instead of a full compression pass).
   - Never worse than not using it, by design.

5. HX Complete (several complete routes, the split route, specialist hand-off)
   What it does: compresses several whole ways and keeps the smallest. The split route sends text and tables to a
   general compressor in one piece, and pictures, sound and sensor data to the Exchange lines.
   Gains, measured against the best other tool on the same data:
   - SQLite database: 6.23x vs 7-Zip 4.64x (25.5% smaller).
   - Screenshots: 50.0x vs PPMd 36.2x (27.6% smaller).
   - Canterbury: 8.03x vs BSC 6.14x (23.5% smaller).
   - Logs, JSON and CSV: 10.21x vs PPMd 8.69x (14.9% smaller).
   - Calgary: 4.58x vs bzip3 4.05x (11.6% smaller).
   - Silesia: 5.055x vs mzip 4.92x (2.7% smaller).
   - Packet captures: 1.413x vs Brotli 1.398x (1.1% smaller).
   - Programs: 3.53x vs 7-Zip 3.50x (0.8% smaller).
   - Speech WAV: 1.985x vs WavPack 1.979x (0.3% smaller).

6. HX Fast (decode-budgeted, with the H Sampler)
   What it does: the same machinery limited to methods that open fast. A quick sample decides what is worth
   running in full, and nothing is chosen from an estimate alone.
   Gains:
   - The H Sampler cut compression time about 8x, with no measurable size loss.
   - HX Fast decodes at up to about 300 MB/s.

7. H-Direct (the learned router)
   What it does: remembers which methods won on files like this one, tries the best three first, and escalates
   when a result misses its prediction.
   Gains (graded on files it never trained on):
   - 26% of the route-trial time saved, for only 0.074% larger output.
   - Its first pick was the true best on 43 of 83 files.

8. Image recovery in H-Deflate
   What it does: unpacks pictures that were stored compressed inside PNG, PDF and Office files back to plain pixels,
   so a real image codec can handle them, and rebuilds the original file exactly on the way back.
   Gains (against rebuilding the deflate streams alone, same compressor behind):
   - PNG: 16.8% smaller (10,439,045 vs 12,549,818 bytes).
   - Office files: 5.7% smaller.
   - PDF: 0.5% smaller (1.4% smaller than precomp).
   - Archives: the same size as precomp, and 30% faster to decode.

9. Harrington Ultimate (per-piece predictive routing with escalation)
   What it does: predicts per piece what each tool will reach and gives misses a second look.
   Gains: the per-piece build is in development. The file-level form is H-Direct, above.

10. Router pre-training
    What it does: trains the router on tens of thousands of public test-data pieces, graded only on files held
    out of training.
    Gains: about 19% of the data could be handed to fast-decoding tools at no size cost. The first trained model is
    not yet better than the best single tool, and I state that openly.

    Overall result of Part A: best in 120 of 290 categories (29 data types x 10 speed tiers), second in 119.


PART B - OPEN SOURCE (MIT unless noted; on GitHub)

Harrington's Sets / hs_zstd (parallel decode)
  - Decodes 2.14x faster than zstd and 1.40x faster than pzstd on average over the 2-10 GB tests (best case
    2.99x faster than zstd), at nearly the same ratio.

HCM (my context-mixing compressor)
  - Canterbury: smallest of all 14 tools tested (350,326 bytes), ahead of ZPAQ -m5.
  - Synthetic set: smaller than ZPAQ -m5, and 3.5x faster to decode.
  - Seismic data: 1.48x smaller than the standard Steim2 format.
  - Synthetic bank data: smallest of every tool tested.
  - HCM Sets mode on plant data: 1.7% smaller than single-stream HCM, and 1.94x faster to decode.

Record-length detection + delta (front end for standard tools)
  - X-ray with zstd -19: 12.1% smaller and 18% faster to decode.
  - Canterbury kennedy.xls with zstd: 15.89x -> 39.01x.
  - X-ray in ORC (zlib): 23.9% smaller and 1.8x faster to read.

The -hi tools (my settings search for a standard encoder; the stock decoder opens the files)
  - 7z-hi:
    - Silesia: 2.9% smaller and 5% faster to decode.
    - ooffice: 16.5% smaller and 1.25x faster.
    - nci: 17.1% smaller and 1.20x faster.
    - Canterbury: 6.2% smaller.
  - lzma-hi: Silesia 1.3% smaller and faster to decode; nci 17.1% smaller.
  - xz-hi: modern log/JSON/CSV set 0.3% smaller and 7.5% faster to decode; 16-bit audio 13.5% smaller.
  - jxl-hi / JPEG-LS tuned: x-ray 2.3% smaller and 1.35x faster to decode; JPEG-LS 0.5% smaller and 1.18x faster.
  - FLAC tuned: speech 2.1% smaller and 9.6% faster to decode.
  - zstd-hi / Brotli-hi: 0.05-1.2% smaller (small but free); zstd-hi compressed 2x faster on one large export.

lpaq1-h (GPL, built on Matt Mahoney's lpaq1)
  - Canterbury: 8.2% smaller than lpaq1, at the same decode speed.
  - Synthetic set: smallest of every tool tested.

zpaq-hi (ZPAQ's own decoder opens it)
  - Canterbury: 0.7% smaller (slower to decode, so a size-only gain).

lzav-hi (encoder for Aleksey Vaneev's LZAV)
  - Silesia: 3.432x vs 2.894x (15.7% smaller); about 14% slower to decode.

H-Deflate v1.01 fast buffer
  - Up to 1.59x faster inflate, with identical output (678 of 678 zlib settings checked bit-exact).

hs_shark (parallel Wireshark processing)
  - 2.9x faster than plain tshark, with identical output on all 964,896 packets.

hs_sieve (AGPL, a modification of Sieve of Potentia)
  - sao: 0.31% smaller and 1.33x faster to decode.

PDF stream rewrite
  - 0.6% smaller than qpdf's known optimisation, and renders faster.

Where things did not help, that is recorded too: record delta on text and machine code, the activity map
(smaller but slower), the gzip decode claim (rapidgzip is faster), and ZPAQ -m4 / BSC beating HCM in their tiers.
