SPEED TIER STANDARD - TIER 1 SPLIT (additional standard)
Michael Hugh Harrington, 9 November 2026. Copyright (c) 2026 Michael Hugh Harrington. MIT License.
Open to suggestions for alternative means.

WHY
The speed tiers place every compressor by its decode time divided by LZ4 -1's decode time on the same files
(M = T_decode(program) / T_decode(LZ4 -1)), with tier limits 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 2000 and any speed.
Tier 1 (M <= 2) holds far too wide a range of speeds: on the Silesia corpus it held programs from M = 0.38 to M = 1.70,
more than a four-fold spread, so the fastest decoders were judged against programs four times slower. This standard
splits Tier 1 into five sub-tiers. Tiers 2 to 10 do not change.

1. THE SUB-TIERS (Renard R5 preferred numbers, ISO 3)
   The limits are the R5 series of ISO 3 (preferred numbers, steps of the fifth root of 10, about 1.6x), the standard
   engineering series for dividing a range into equal geometric steps. They are fixed by the standard, not by any result.
   Tier 1A   M <= 0.4
   Tier 1B   M <= 0.63
   Tier 1C   M <= 1.0      (as fast as LZ4 -1 or faster)
   Tier 1D   M <= 1.6
   Tier 1E   M <= 2.0      (the old Tier 1 limit)
   Like the main tiers, the sub-tiers are cumulative: a program in 1A also counts in 1B to 1E and in tiers 2 to 10.

2. BORDER ITEMS (guard band, ISO 14253-1; uncertainty by GUM Type B evaluation, JCGM 100:2008)
   A program whose M is above a sub-tier limit by no more than the guard band also counts in that sub-tier, and is
   marked as a border item. The guard band is the measurement uncertainty of the decode times, evaluated as GUM
   "Type B" (from information already in the data, without new runs):
     guard band = 2.6%: the widest gap between the three LZ4 levels (-1, -9, -12), which all use the same LZ4 decoder,
     on the Silesia run below (0.932 s, 0.944 s, 0.957 s; 0.025 / 0.944 = 2.6%).
   This is a cautious (wide) figure, since the three LZ4 files differ slightly in content.

3. THE WIN RULE (unchanged)
   In each sub-tier, the smallest of ours is compared with the smallest rival. WIN when ours is the same size or smaller.

5. LIMITS A REVIEWER SHOULD KNOW
   - Times are wall clock including program start-up and writing the output to disk, so the fastest decoders sit closer
     together than their decoding alone would put them, and LZ4's own time (the yardstick) includes that overhead too.
   - HX Fast decodes on all logical cores; the stock rivals decode on one.
   - The Silesia run was made on a computer that was also being used for gaming; sizes are unaffected, times may be.
