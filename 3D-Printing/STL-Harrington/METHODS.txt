Code: source/stlx3.h (the mesh coder, working name stlx3) and source/stlh_core.h (reading, choosing, packing, checking).

1. READING
Binary STL, ASCII STL and OBJ are read the same way as in Harrington-3MF (see that folder's METHODS.txt), except that every
triangle is kept, including flat ones; STL allows them. Corners with exactly the same 32-bit coordinates become one vertex.

2. THE MESH CODER (stlx3) - "attached to a recent side"
Triangles are coded one after another. The coder keeps a short list of the 32 most recently used sides (edges) and the 16
most recently used corners. Most triangles share a side with a triangle coded just before them, so each triangle is coded as
one small symbol: "attached to recent side number j", where j is usually 0, 1 or 2. Only its third corner is then needed,
and that is one of three things: a corner seen recently (one of the 16), a corner seen long ago (stored as a distance back),
or a new corner. A triangle that touches no recent side is coded as a restart with three corners. Triangles keep their
winding: the shared side is used in the direction the triangle runs.

3. PREDICTING NEW CORNERS - the parallelogram
When a new corner belongs to a triangle attached across a shared side, the coder looks at the triangle on the other side of
that side and predicts the new corner as the fourth point of the parallelogram: shared corner A + shared corner B - the
opposite corner. On smooth surfaces and on the flat strips of printed parts this lands very close. A new corner with no such
neighbour is predicted from the corner coded just before it.

4. STORING THE CORRECTIONS EXACTLY
Each coordinate is turned into an order-keeping 32-bit integer (the float's bits rearranged so that larger numbers give larger
integers, negative numbers included). The correction is the difference between the true integer and the predicted integer,
folded so that small negative and small positive differences both become small numbers (zigzag). Adding the correction back
gives exactly the original bits, so the geometry is exact, not rounded. The corrections are split into byte planes: for each
axis, all the top bytes together, then all the second bytes, and so on, so that the packer sees long runs of similar bytes.

5. LIKE WITH LIKE - THREE TRIANGLE ORDERS, BEST KEPT
The same mesh is coded in three orders: the file's own order; a neighbour pre-sort (meshoptimizer's vertex-cache order, so
triangles that share corners sit together); and a side walk (a depth-first walk from triangle to triangle across shared
sides). Each result is packed quickly with zstd level 3, and the order that packs smallest is kept.

6. PACKING
The kept block (with the original 80-byte STL header in front, for binary STL) is packed with ZPAQ method 5 ("Smallest") or
zstd level 19 ("Fast"). The .stlh header stores the unpacked size and a CRC-32 (see STLH-FILE-FORMAT.txt).

7. SELF-CHECK
Before saving, the finished .stlh is unpacked, its CRC-32 checked, the mesh decoded, and the decoded triangles compared with
the original: each triangle turned to its smallest starting corner (keeping winding), all sorted, and compared bit for bit.
If anything differs, nothing is saved.

8. RESTORING
The .stlh is unpacked, checked against its CRC-32 and decoded, and a binary STL is written: the kept 80-byte header (or a
short label), the triangle count, and for each triangle a normal worked out from its corners (cross product, made unit
length), the three corners exactly, and a zero attribute.
