CREDITS
Harrington's Insights, Michael Hugh Harrington, 29 October 2026

Much of this work is close to the work of others and is built on it, and that work is gladly credited here.

HCM (Harrington Compression Method) is a working name for a build whose overall design follows Matt
Mahoney's lpaq1: hashed contexts of the previous one to eight bytes, word contexts, a long-match model, a
mixer whose weights are chosen by the partial byte and the match length, and an APM refinement stage. The
record contexts added in v3.3 and HCM-Bin are related to the paq8 record model, which detects fixed-length
records from a byte recurring at equal spacing and adds contexts from the rows above. The hash-table layout
(64-byte buckets with check values) and the adaptive counters, which adapt at a rate of 1/(n + 1.6), follow
lpaq and ZPAQ (ZPAQ uses 1/(n + 1.5)). The arithmetic coder follows Witten, Neal and Cleary (1987). The
exp() and log() functions used to build HCM's startup tables come from Arm Optimized Routines.

lpaq1-h is a mutant of Matt Mahoney's lpaq1 (GPL). zpaq-hi is Matt Mahoney's ZPAQ / libzpaq with added
candidate models. The -hi tools (xz-hi, lzma-hi, 7z-hi, zip-hi, bzip2-hi, zstd-hi, Brotli-hi, jxl-hi,
flac-hi) are the original authors' software with marked encoder-side changes; their output is read by the
stock decoders. Each keeps its original license and notices.

Michael Hugh Harrington's own contributions are the additions built on these engines (record detection
driving stock tools, the ternary pointer, word-position and line-position contexts, match recovery and the
other changes listed in the book), the Harrington Exchange, Combary, HaBiTS, the header methods, and
Harrington Sets. They are released under the MIT License and offered free for use in the original tools.
