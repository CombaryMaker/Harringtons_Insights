BANK AND SEISMIC DATA TESTS - 1 November 2026 (cloud machine, 2 cores). Michael Hugh Harrington. MIT License.
Every decode compared byte for byte with the original; all exact.

SEISMIC (real public data): 8 station-day recordings from EarthScope (FDSN dataselect), 2024-01-01, the day of the
M7.5 Noto earthquake: IU.MAJO (Japan), IU.ANMO, IU.COLA, IU.KONO, IU.TATO, II.PFO (BHZ) and IU.MAJO, IU.ANMO (HHZ).
Archive format Steim2 in miniSEED; decoded to 132,250,680 bytes of 32-bit samples with steim.c (every record's
reverse integration constant checked). SHA-256 of the downloaded files in seismic-data-sha256.txt.

HCM on the raw samples is 1.48x smaller than Steim2 and beats every other method, but decodes about 370x slower
than Steim2: an archive-storage result, not a live-data one. Record delta picked a record length of 3 bytes for
4-byte samples and made the data larger; seismic samples are not fixed-layout records (its size-and-speed check
would switch it off).

BANK (synthetic data in public formats, gen_bank.py; no real people, accounts or banks): 180 NACHA ACH files
(94-character records; payroll and bill-payment batches; routing numbers random with valid ABA check digits) and
360 ATM electronic journals (12 ATMs x 30 days). Three tar archives: ACH only, journals only, and a mixed daily backup.

HCM is the smallest on all three (22%, 47% and 28% smaller than the best standard tool) at a large decode-time cost.
Record delta made every bank archive larger: ACH records are mostly random fields (accounts, amounts, names), and
the journals are variable-length text lines; its size-and-speed check would switch it off.

On the mixed bank backup, Sets at 16 MB was smaller than plain blocks (1.4%) and smaller than single-stream zstd
(1.0%) while compressing faster than zstd -T2; decode on 2 cores was slower than zstd's (0.30 s vs 0.16 s). On the
seismic data Sets and plain blocks tie: quiet and quake stretches do not differ enough in byte statistics.
Timings are from a shared 2-core cloud machine; sizes are exact.
