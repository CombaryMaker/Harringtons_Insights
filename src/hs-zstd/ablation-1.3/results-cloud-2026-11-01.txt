Findings (agree with Andrew's single-core size results):
1. Sets as published (entropy raw rule) lose to plain block splitting on both stress files: +8.8-11.3% on switch_1mb, +30.7% on dup_random_chunks (whole file stored raw).
2. The raw rule is the cause, not the grouping. Sets with --raw off beat plain block splitting by 9.0% (16 MB) / 5.9% (4 MB) on switch_1mb and by 4.4% / 1.4% on dup_random_chunks.
3. On Silesia joined, Sets are neutral: every variant within 0.18% of --sets 0.
4. --raw test recovers most of the switch_1mb loss but not the duplicate-chunk case (trial window cannot see later repeats).
5. --smooth safe alone changes nothing on switch_1mb/Silesia and rescues dup_random_chunks (-0.1% instead of +30.7%); combined with --raw off it is identical to --raw off alone.
6. zstd -3 --long=27 wins outright on dup_random_chunks (21.7 MB vs 36.8 MB best block-split) because the repeats are further apart than one block; on switch_1mb it is 9% worse than Sets --raw off.
Decision pending: make --raw off + --smooth safe the default in hs_zstd 1.3 (format unchanged, old files still decode).
