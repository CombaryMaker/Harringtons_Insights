INDUSTRIAL PLANT-DATA EXPORT TEST - 1 November 2026 (cloud machine, 2 cores). Michael Hugh Harrington. MIT License.
REAL public data in one archive (plant.tar, 163,870,720 bytes), as a plant data export or backup would be:
  UCI SECOM (semiconductor fab sensor readings), UCI condition monitoring of hydraulic systems (PS1 pressure at
  100 Hz, flow, temperature, vibration, cooling, motor power; PS2-PS6 and EPS1 left out to keep the run short),
  UCI appliances energy (building sensors), UCI steel industry energy, UCI air quality (includes an .xlsx, which is
  already compressed), Tennessee Eastman chemical plant benchmark (Braatz data), Numenta NAB machine and ambient
  temperature. Download hashes in data-sha256.txt. Every decode compared byte for byte; all exact.

FINDINGS
- HCM is the smallest: 9.27 to 1, 19% smaller than xz -9 (the best standard tool), at a large decode-time cost.
- NOTABLE: HCM in Harrington Sets mode was SMALLER than single-stream HCM (17,675,237 vs 17,987,330 bytes) and
  2.65x faster to compress and 1.94x faster to decode on 2 cores. Grouping the different sensor files into sets
  with their own models beat one model learning everything in a row - usually Sets costs a little size.
- With zstd behind it, Sets tied plain blocks (4 MB) or was 0.2% larger (16 MB): these files are mostly text tables
  of numbers with similar byte statistics, so the zstd grouping found little to separate.
Timings are from a shared 2-core cloud machine; sizes are exact.
